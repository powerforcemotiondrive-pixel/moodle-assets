GSEA Semester 1 Lesson Outline

Session 1: Travel, Transport & Leisure

Essential professional and general vocabulary covering modern transit systems, ticketing mechanics, leisure tourism infrastructure, and advanced narrative structures.

Itinerary

01

A planned route or journey, detailing scheduled stops, transport connections, and activities.

Layover

02

A period of waiting between two connecting flights or segments of a travel journey.

Commuter Pass

03

A discounted ticket valid for multiple journeys over a specified period on public transport.

Baggage Allowance

04

The maximum weight and volume of luggage a passenger can check in or carry on without penalty.

Hub-and-Spoke

05

A transport distribution network where traffic flows through a central hub airport or station.

Round-Trip

06

A journey to a destination and back to the original starting location.

Code-Share

07

An airline partnership where multiple carriers market and sell seats on the same operating flight.

Transit Lounge

08

A designated area within an airport terminal for passengers waiting to catch connecting flights.

All-Inclusive

09

A vacation package where meals, drinks, and resort leisure activities are bundled into one price.

Car Rental Agency

10

A commercial enterprise providing short-term vehicle leases to leisure and business travelers.

Hop-On Hop-Off

11

A flexible tourist transport service allowing passengers to board and exit at designated spots.

Jet Lag

12

Fatigue and sleep disruption resulting from rapid long-distance travel across multiple time zones.

Excursion

13

A short leisure trip or outing taken by tourists during a vacation, usually returning the same day.

Peak Season

14

The time of year when travel demand is highest, resulting in premium prices and crowded venues.

Guided Tour

15

A structured group excursion led by an expert interpreter or local tourism specialist.

Boutique Hotel

16

A small stylish accommodation provider known for intimate ambience and personalized leisure services.

Overbooking

17

Selling more reservations than available seats or rooms to account for potential cancellations.

Red-Eye Flight

18

An overnight flight that departs late in the evening and arrives early the following morning.

Travel Insurance

19

Coverage purchased to protect against unforeseen expenses such as trip cancellations or emergencies.

Boarding Pass

20

A document provided by an airline granting permission to enter the secure boarding area and aircraft.

Frequent Flyer

21

A loyalty program offered by airlines to reward passengers with redeemable miles and exclusive perks.

Sightseeing

22

The leisure activity of visiting places of interest, historical landmarks, and scenic attractions.

Customs Declaration

23

An official statement detailing goods or currency being brought across an international border.

Connecting Flight

24

A flight requiring passengers to change planes at an intermediate stop before reaching their final destination.

Local Transit

25

Public transport networks including buses, trams, and subways serving metropolitan and tourist areas.

Tourism Board

26

An official organization promoting regional leisure destinations and providing visitor information.

Platform Screen

27

Automated digital displays at railway and subway platforms indicating train arrivals and schedules.

Departure Board

28

A centralized electronic board listing flight or train schedules, gate changes, and delays.

Terminal Shuttle

29

A complimentary vehicle or train transporting passengers between different airport or station terminals.

Express Check-In

30

Accelerated ticketing service enabling travelers to bypass long lines using mobile apps or kiosks.

Travel & Transport Context Statements (Past Perfect Tense Focus)

  • • By the time the passenger arrived at the transit lounge, the airline had already updated the departure board with a gate change.
  • • Before they booked their all-inclusive holiday package, they had carefully reviewed several boutique hotel itineraries online.
  • • The tourist realized that she had left her commuter pass inside the car rental agency right after she passed the customs declaration checkpoint.
  • • Because the airline had overbooked the red-eye flight, several frequent flyers had received vouchers for alternative routing.
  • • Prior to joining the guided sightseeing tour, the travelers had already purchased their tickets using an express check-in kiosk.

Grammar Focus: The Past Perfect Tense (had + past participle)

The past perfect tense is used to talk about an action that happened before another action in the past. In travel and transport narratives, it clarifies the precise sequence of events:

  • Structure: Subject + had + past participle (e.g., had booked, had arrived, had departed).
  • Sequence Clarification: It establishes which past event occurred first (e.g., "When we reached the station, the train had already left").
  • Professional Application: Essential for incident reports, flight delay explanations, and detailing travel itineraries during business communications.
